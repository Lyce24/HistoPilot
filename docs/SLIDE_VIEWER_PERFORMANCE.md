# Slide preparation and progressive viewing

The intended flow is **select a slide → prepare it → explore**. Preparation opens the reader, displays an overview, and warms useful whole-slide zoom levels before reporting **Slide ready**. The overview remains usable during preparation. Errors show partial preparation and a retry action rather than an endless loading state or a false ready message.

## Why details were slow

The previous image path waited 180 ms after the last camera change, then requested a fresh arbitrary viewport crop at up to 1536 pixels on its longest side. Neighboring views had different cache keys. Each SDPC request launched an interpreter, rediscovered native libraries, opened the slide, rendered the crop, and encoded a PNG. Continuous navigation could keep postponing detail requests.

The native reader already used the slide pyramid; the missing pieces were stable tile reuse, progressive scheduling, warm readers, and cheaper lossless encoding.

## Current behavior and limits

- **Preparation:** three complete virtual pyramid levels, chosen so their combined coverage fits at most 128 tiles. This prepares useful overview/nearby detail, not every native-resolution pixel. Preparation progress is tied to the selected slide and resets on source changes.
- **Tiles:** at most 64 visible tiles preserve detail in wide high-DPI views while keeping oversized requests bounded. Fixed 512-pixel tiles on a power-of-two grid in level-0 coordinates. OpenSlide/OpenSDPC chooses an appropriate native level. Edge tiles are clipped to the actual slide dimensions. The existing authenticated region APIs still validate frozen slide membership and source identity.
- **Source identity:** QC geometry returns a source fingerprint. Overview, tile, exact-patch and patch-geometry requests send it back, and the server rejects a changed file before rendering. The browser keys prepared images and selections by that fingerprint, including for legacy datasets without saved source stamps. A changed source clears the old canvas, crops and review selection. Reload slide creates a new viewing session and refetches geometry, overview and feature evidence, rather than retrying an obsolete fingerprint. Modern frozen-inventory mismatches remain blocked. Optional feature overlays must match the current geometry and fingerprint.
- **Scheduling:** at most two active fetch/decode operations per viewer. Visible tiles take priority; camera updates replace obsolete queued work at intervals of at most 80 ms rather than waiting for gestures to finish. After 200 ms without camera changes, up to 24 tiles anticipate nearby panning and the next centered zoom. Visible requests take priority over preparation, which takes priority over prediction. At most one speculative read runs, preserving the other slot for navigation. Obsolete queued predictions are discarded; attempted predictions cannot enter an eviction/refetch loop. Existing reads complete without a cancel/relaunch storm. Changing slides or unmounting aborts outstanding frontend requests. Fetch plus image decoding has a 30-second deadline; timed-out slots recover, late results are discarded, and manual retries preserve valid cached pixels.
- **Camera:** ordinary coarse mouse-wheel steps ease over a bounded 120 ms while maintaining the cursor anchor. Fine trackpad/pinch input follows the gesture directly. Wheel classification stays consistent during a gesture burst (180 ms idle reset). Capturing pointer-down stops any wheel animation before drag coordinates are recorded, including a published but not yet committed camera update. Reduced-motion preferences disable easing. Panning publishes at most once per animation frame and flushes the final position on release. Fit/selection/source changes cancel obsolete motion.
- **Display:** cached tile selection follows every camera frame independently of the 80 ms request scheduler, so panning into an already prepared tile does not wait for a network-queue update. The overview and already decoded tiles remain in their original coordinates while sharper tiles arrive. Cached lower-resolution tiles render beneath incomplete finer coverage. Once the desired level is complete, redundant coarser layers are removed from the rendered view. Finer prefetched tiles remain offscreen until needed; fitting the slide does not draw the entire preparation cache. Review regions, patch coverage and attention remain in the same level-0 coordinate system. Attention-map calculation still has its own scheduling; it does not gate image detail.
- **QC overlays:** recorded patch coverage and tissue contours are prepared as a whole-slide image plus at most one sharper viewport image, each capped at 2048 pixels per side. Viewport sharpening is throttled to 120 ms and geometry processing yields in approximately 4 ms batches. The previous buffer retains its own level-0 position while sharpening; an even-odd clip prevents double-opacity overlap. Contour holes remain empty. Patch picking uses original coordinates and paint order; selected patches and review regions remain vector geometry. Encoding plus decoding has a 10-second deadline with cancellation cleanup.
- **Attention overlays:** dense patch sorting and painting yield between approximately 4 ms work batches and cancel obsolete work. Completed stable weight orderings are cached by patch-array identity. PNG creation remains asynchronous; the previous heatmap stays at its own original slide coordinates until its replacement is ready. Crop sidebars are memoized independently of camera motion.
- **Browser memory:** each open viewer retains up to 192 cached tiles and 256 MiB of accounted decoded pixels plus encoded blobs. Eviction prefers speculative or offscreen detail over the current view and the two coarser prepared fallback levels, then uses recency within each priority. Cached tiles remain available while the viewer is open; they do not expire merely because the user pauses to inspect tissue. Source changes, authentication/source failures, or closing the viewer invalidate the relevant cache and revoke image URLs. Transient reader-busy errors retain valid cached content.
- **Server image cache:** up to 128 MiB / 512 lossless PNG results shared across authorized requests and viewer reopens. Concurrent identical requests share one render. Entries expire after ten minutes idle, checked on cache access, and are cleared at shutdown. Source file stamps are checked before and after every access, including hits; Reader-runtime identity for either native backend also participates in the key. At most 64 distinct render keys are pending, with a 25-second bound on duplicate-request waits.
- **Native readers:** OpenSDPC, OpenSlide and the bounded Pillow fallback share at most two isolated child processes. Each retains one slide and recycles after 128 requests, five minutes of age, or 60 seconds idle. Native import/open/read and IPC share a 20-second deadline; request writes are also bounded. Native stdout cannot corrupt the acknowledgement channel. Each request checks source stamps before and after reading. A timeout or crash retires the child so subsequent requests can recover. No native slide open/read runs inside the API process. On supported POSIX runtimes, the existing 4 GiB address-space and 24 MiB output-file limits remain.
- **OpenSlide cache:** within its isolated child, an OpenSlide 4-compatible reader receives a 32 MiB decoded-tile cache; older libraries retain their built-in cache. SDPC uses the configured TRIDENT/OpenSDPC environment; OpenSlide and raster fallback use the API Python environment with its optional imaging dependencies.
- **Images:** PNG remains lossless, using compression level 1. This reduces encoding CPU cost without changing image pixels. The existing 2048-pixel output-side and 32-megapixel intermediate-read limits remain. No whole-slide conversion, feature re-extraction or model change is required.

“Ready” means the bounded preparation completed. Unvisited higher-resolution areas can still require reads. A 250480 × 142080 slide contains approximately 35.6 billion level-0 pixels; uncompressed RGB alone is roughly 106.8 GB. Preparing every pixel in browser memory would be a poor default.

## Local measurements

The later [five-slide browser/API acceptance report](REAL_SLIDE_VIEWER_VALIDATION.md) tests genuine SDPC, TIFF and SVS images, including a 6.19 GB slide, at DPR 1 and 2. Its 73 checks passed. It is the current practical viewing verdict; the earlier isolated measurements below describe individual optimizations.

A short benchmark used the existing GEJ SDPC slide (250480 × 142080, eight pyramid levels) on this workstation. It used the configured TRIDENT reader environment and did not start the HistoPilot server.

| Measurement | Earlier path | Warm readers + PNG level 1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three matching 1536-pixel tissue views | 1.20, 1.57, 1.86 s | 0.57, 0.49, 0.46 s on a second pass |
| PNG encoding for those views | 0.40, 0.85, 1.04 s | 0.14, 0.16, 0.16 s |
| Six 512-pixel tissue tiles | Not measured | Median 230 ms; range 179–274 ms |

Cold metadata/reader opening took 582 ms in the tile benchmark. The sampled child retained roughly 96–104 MiB RSS. Measurements cover one slide and local rendering, with operating-system caches warm; they exclude browser and network latency and do not establish production tail latency. The raw local report is `/tmp/histopilot-slide-performance-20260925.json`.

A follow-up with six 512-pixel SDPC tissue tiles measured 110–142 ms per uncached tile after a 426 ms cold first request, versus **4.9 ms median (3.8–6.2 ms)** through the encoded-image cache. Cached results were byte-identical. The shared cache held 1.59 MB for those six tiles. PNG level-1 encoding took 21 ms on one sample; lossless WebP took 38 ms at method 0 and 139 ms at method 3, so the viewer retains PNG. These are local render/cache measurements, excluding HTTP, browser decoding and compositing; they do not imply every new tissue area loads in 5 ms. Report: `/tmp/histopilot-slide-image-cache-20260925.json`.

A separate headless Chromium test rendered 100,000 synthetic attention patches into a 2048 × 1366 heatmap. The previous synchronous path caused a 385 ms maximum observed animation-frame gap. Cooperative batches reduced this to 21.5 ms on the first render and 22.6 ms with cached ordering, with zero differing pixel channels. Total first-render work took 702 ms spread across 45 frames; cached-order rendering took 406 ms across 27 frames. This improves responsiveness, not total raster CPU time, and is not a hardware-independent frame-rate guarantee. Report: `/tmp/histopilot-attention-performance-20260925.json`.

A dense QC overlay benchmark compared 4,096 patch rectangles plus 50,000 contour vertices with the new cached raster layer. In two 118-frame navigation runs per mode, SVG geometry produced **11 frames over 25 ms out of 236**; the prepared raster produced **zero out of 236**, with a 16.8 ms maximum gap. Preparing the 2048 × 1365 raster took 130.5 ms wall time, spread over input-friendly batches with a 16.7 ms maximum observed frame gap. This isolated synthetic software-rendered Chromium benchmark measures overlay composition, not the complete React viewer, native slide decoding or QuPath side by side. Report: `/tmp/histopilot-qc-overlay-profile/results.json`.

A real SDPC smoke test after native-reader unification verified 250480 × 142080 geometry and byte-identical cached pixels: metadata 342 ms, one 512-pixel tile 90 ms, repeat 7.5 ms. This is one selected region, not a representative latency distribution; it excludes HTTP and browser work. Report: `/tmp/histopilot-qupath-reader-smoke.json`.

Offline browser verification covers preparing→ready, partial preparation/retry, cached first zoom, overlap reuse during pan, detail requests during continuous zoom, concurrency bounds, stale queue replacement, source switching, review-coordinate alignment, and wheel behavior inside/outside the viewer, wheel-to-drag scale continuity, dense raster patch picking and ROI coordinates, overlay toggles, stale-source reload, frozen-source rejection and restored-source recovery. Scheduler tests additionally cover predictive priority, one speculative reader, prediction replacement, bounded cache memory, preserved coarse fallback and avoiding unnecessary image layers, immediate cached painting during throttled requests, hung fetch/decode cleanup and source-disposal races. Reader/cache tests cover reuse, shared in-flight renders, mutation checks on cache hits, native import/open/read failure and timeout recovery for both OpenSDPC and OpenSlide, bounded concurrency, expiry, shutdown and spatial alignment.

The final verification pass for this change completed 780 frontend tests, 179 relevant backend/API tests, and 131 offline browser checks with no browser exceptions. Type checking, production build, Python lint and patch whitespace checks passed; all 84 packaged frontend files matched the build by SHA-256. ## Relationship to QuPath and HALO

QuPath's region store loads tiles asynchronously, deduplicates pending reads, and maintains a bounded decoded-image cache. Its renderer draws cached lower-resolution tiles or the thumbnail while finer data loads. These mechanisms are visible in the primary [region-store source](https://github.com/qupath/qupath/blob/main/qupath-gui-fx/src/main/java/qupath/lib/gui/images/stores/AbstractImageRegionStore.java) and [rendering source](https://github.com/qupath/qupath/blob/main/qupath-gui-fx/src/main/java/qupath/lib/gui/images/stores/DefaultImageRegionStore.java).

Indica Labs documents faster HALO viewing with pyramidal TIFF images in its [application note](https://cdn.indicalab.com/wp-content/uploads/20230419153547/Vectra3Fusion-and-Analysis-App-Note.pdf). Its exact cache/scheduler implementation is proprietary and was not verified. HistoPilot follows the documented tiled, progressive pattern; it does not claim to reproduce HALO internals or match native desktop performance on every machine.
